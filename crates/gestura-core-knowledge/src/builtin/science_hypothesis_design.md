# Science: Hypothesis & Study Design

Use this module when the task is translating a scientific question into a falsifiable hypothesis, selecting variables and controls, or designing a study with clear measurements and decision logic.

## Focus

- Scientific questions should become explicit, falsifiable claims.
- Study design should distinguish plausible competing explanations.
- Reproducibility, bias control, and ethics are part of study quality, not afterthoughts.

## High-Value Defaults

- State the hypothesis in falsifiable terms.
- Identify independent variables, dependent variables, controls, confounders, and measurement methods.
- Separate exploratory work from confirmatory work.
- Prefer designs that increase information gain per experiment instead of broad low-signal activity.
- Note ethical, safety, or reproducibility limits when relevant.

## Review Lens

- Does the design isolate the variable of interest?
- Are the chosen measurements valid proxies for the phenomenon being studied?
- Which result would falsify the current explanation?
- Are blinding, randomization, calibration, or sampling concerns material here?

## Common Failure Modes

- Posing a hypothesis too vaguely to test.
- Measuring convenience proxies instead of the real phenomenon.
- Designing a study that cannot distinguish mechanism from association.
- Leaving confounders or sampling bias implicit.

## Good Outputs

- Hypothesis tables with variables, controls, and expected outcomes.
- Study plans with measurements, decision rules, and key risks.
- Prioritized follow-up experiments based on information gain.
- Lists of unresolved assumptions and reproducibility constraints.
