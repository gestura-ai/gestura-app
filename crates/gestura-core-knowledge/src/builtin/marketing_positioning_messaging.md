# Marketing: Positioning & Messaging

Use this module when the task is defining the ideal customer profile, sharpening the value proposition, clarifying differentiation, or building a message hierarchy that sales and marketing can actually use.

## Focus

- Positioning must start from audience reality, not internal product language.
- Messaging should connect pain, promised outcome, proof, and call to action coherently.
- Strong messaging includes what the offer is for, why it matters now, and why it is credible.

## High-Value Defaults

- Start from audience pain, urgency, and differentiated value.
- Make every message answer “why this, why now, why trust us?”.
- Align positioning, objections, proof, and CTA.
- Prefer a clear message house over a long feature list.
- Tie messaging to a measurable funnel objective.

## Review Lens

- Can the intended audience immediately recognize themselves in the framing?
- Is the offer contrasted clearly against the status quo or alternatives?
- What proof points make the claim believable?
- Which objections would block conversion if left unanswered?

## Common Failure Modes

- Writing copy that could belong to any competitor.
- Confusing feature lists with positioning.
- Ignoring the buyer's current workaround or default choice.
- Creating messages product or sales cannot support consistently.

## Good Outputs

- ICP and message-house summaries.
- Positioning statements with explicit contrast to alternatives.
- Objection-handling notes by buyer concern.
- Messaging recommendations by channel or funnel stage.
