# Chemical Engineering: Balances & Thermodynamics

Use this module when the task is reasoning about mass balance, energy balance, phase behavior, equilibrium, or the data needed to trust a process concept.

## Focus

- Governing balances should be written before process optimization begins.
- Feed assumptions, operating windows, and thermodynamic models determine whether conclusions are trustworthy.
- Feasibility and efficiency should be kept separate in the analysis.

## High-Value Defaults

- Write the governing balances before optimizing the process.
- Make feed assumptions, operating windows, and equilibrium limits explicit.
- Separate feasibility from efficiency.
- Note where the chosen model is sensitive to assumptions.
- Summarize what data is still required to trust the process view.

## Review Lens

- Are all relevant streams, recycle loops, and loss terms represented?
- Which phase-equilibrium or property assumptions dominate the conclusion?
- Is the claimed benefit actually limited by mass balance, energy balance, or equilibrium?
- What data is assumed rather than measured?

## Common Failure Modes

- Optimizing before validating the governing balances.
- Confusing thermodynamic feasibility with attractive economics.
- Ignoring impurities, recycles, or trace contaminants that change the balance.
- Using property models outside their credible range.

## Good Outputs

- Balance summaries with key stream assumptions.
- Thermodynamic reasoning notes and model caveats.
- Lists of missing property or operating data.
- Recommendations for the next validation calculation or experiment.

## Retrieval Hints

mass balance, energy balance, thermodynamics, equilibrium, phase behavior, property model, recycle stream, operating window, process feasibility.
