# Mechanical Engineering: Manufacturing & Reliability

Use this module when the task is evaluating manufacturability, assembly sequence, reliability risks, wear mechanisms, or maintenance and inspection planning.

## Focus

- Manufacturing route and assembly sequence shape what is practical and reliable.
- Reliability work should consider fatigue, wear, corrosion, looseness, and service access early.
- Verification should cover both prototype feasibility and production variation risk.

## High-Value Defaults

- Consider manufacturing route and assembly sequence early.
- Highlight fatigue, wear, corrosion, thermal expansion, and service access.
- Prefer realistic tolerance expectations over idealized drawings.
- Include likely failure modes and inspection points.
- End with a verification plan for prototype and production risk.

## Review Lens

- Which process capabilities and inspection limits constrain the design?
- How will assembly order, fixturing, and tool access influence quality?
- Where will fatigue, fretting, wear, or corrosion accumulate over life?
- Can field teams inspect, maintain, and replace the relevant parts?

## Common Failure Modes

- Treating manufacturability as a late-stage optimization.
- Assuming prototype success guarantees production robustness.
- Specifying tolerances that vendors can neither hold nor measure affordably.
- Ignoring service access until maintenance becomes expensive or unsafe.

## Good Outputs

- Manufacturing-route comparisons with risk notes.
- Assembly and inspection checklists.
- Reliability reviews keyed to fatigue, wear, and environmental stress.
- Verification plans for pilot builds and life testing.
