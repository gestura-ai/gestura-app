# Civil Engineering: Site & Drainage

Use this module when the task is evaluating grading, drainage, runoff behavior, site layout, or the constraints imposed by topography, utilities, and jurisdiction.

## Focus

- Water movement and site conditions govern feasibility.
- Grading, access, maintenance, and utility conflicts should be reasoned about together.
- Conceptual guidance must remain clearly distinct from stamped engineering deliverables.

## High-Value Defaults

- Start from site conditions, water movement, and jurisdictional constraints.
- Keep access, maintenance, and drainage performance in scope together.
- Surface grading assumptions and downstream impacts early.
- Distinguish conceptual guidance from stamped deliverables.
- End with the key assumptions that require licensed review.

## Review Lens

- How does water enter, move through, and leave the site?
- What topographic, utility, or access conflicts shape the design?
- Which assumptions depend on survey, hydrology, or jurisdiction-specific review?
- How will maintenance and inspection work after construction?

## Common Failure Modes

- Treating drainage as a detail instead of a governing design driver.
- Ignoring utility conflicts or construction access constraints.
- Overstating code compliance without jurisdiction-specific analysis.
- Failing to state which assumptions require licensed review or field confirmation.

## Good Outputs

- Site-constraint and drainage summaries.
- Grading and runoff reasoning notes.
- Assumption lists for licensed review.
- Recommendations for surveys, hydrology checks, or layout changes.

## Retrieval Hints

site drainage, grading, runoff, stormwater, site layout, utility conflict, topography, access constraint, hydrology, jurisdiction review.
