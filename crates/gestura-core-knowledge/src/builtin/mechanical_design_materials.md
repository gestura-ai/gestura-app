# Mechanical Engineering: Design & Materials

Use this module when the task is selecting materials, shaping geometry, defining tolerances, or reasoning about how loads and environment should drive a mechanical design.

## Focus

- Mechanical design starts with real loading, duty cycle, environment, and failure consequence.
- Geometry, material, tolerance, and interface decisions are tightly coupled.
- Good design work compares concepts on stiffness, durability, manufacturability, and service burden together.

## High-Value Defaults

- Start with duty cycle, loads, environment, and failure consequences.
- Choose geometry and materials together, not independently.
- Treat tolerances and interfaces as core inputs, not later cleanup.
- Surface assumptions about static vs dynamic loading.
- Include design tradeoffs in weight, stiffness, durability, and cost.

## Review Lens

- What are the true load paths and boundary conditions?
- Which failure modes dominate: yielding, fatigue, buckling, wear, or thermal distortion?
- Are the tolerances achievable and inspectable for the chosen process?
- How do assembly and service interfaces constrain the design?

## Common Failure Modes

- Designing for peak load but ignoring fatigue or vibration.
- Choosing materials from data sheets without environment or process effects.
- Treating tolerances as cleanup work after geometry is fixed.
- Optimizing weight or stiffness without considering manufacturability.

## Good Outputs

- Concept comparisons tied to loads and failure modes.
- Material-selection rationales with process and environment implications.
- Tolerance and interface notes for assembly and inspection.
- Recommendations for analysis, prototyping, or test next.
