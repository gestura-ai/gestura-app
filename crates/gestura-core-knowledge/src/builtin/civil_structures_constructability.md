# Civil Engineering: Structures & Constructability

Use this module when the task is reasoning about load paths, structural options, temporary works, staging, or whether a civil design can be built safely and practically.

## Focus

- Governing loads and load paths should be explicit.
- Constructability and sequencing are part of design quality, not downstream execution details.
- Geotechnical assumptions, staging, access, and maintenance affect the best structural choice.

## High-Value Defaults

- Make governing loads and load paths explicit.
- Treat constructability and sequencing as part of the design.
- Include geotechnical, access, staging, and maintenance implications.
- Avoid implying code compliance without jurisdiction-specific review.
- End with the riskiest assumptions and the needed verification path.

## Review Lens

- What loads govern the design and during which construction or service phase?
- How do access, staging, and temporary support change the feasible options?
- Which geotechnical assumptions matter most to the structural concept?
- What should be verified before implying that the concept is buildable?

## Common Failure Modes

- Checking only final-state loads while ignoring construction-stage conditions.
- Assuming a structurally sound concept is automatically constructible.
- Ignoring maintenance and inspection burdens after handoff.
- Suggesting code compliance without jurisdiction-specific analysis.

## Good Outputs

- Load-path and staging summaries.
- Constructability reviews tied to access and sequence.
- Assumption lists for geotechnical and licensed review.
- Recommendations for the next verification or field check.

## Retrieval Hints

load path, constructability, staging, temporary works, geotechnical assumption, structural concept, construction sequence, access, maintenance burden.
