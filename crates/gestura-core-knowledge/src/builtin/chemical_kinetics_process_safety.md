# Chemical Engineering: Kinetics & Process Safety

Use this module when the task is evaluating reaction rates, separation difficulty, controllability, hazard exposure, or the scale-up risks that determine whether a process is safe and viable.

## Focus

- Kinetic limits, transport limits, and thermodynamic limits should be distinguished clearly.
- Process safety is a design input, not a downstream checklist.
- Lab feasibility should not be mistaken for production viability.

## High-Value Defaults

- Distinguish kinetic limits from thermodynamic limits.
- Include separation difficulty, runaway risk, and control challenges.
- Treat containment, materials compatibility, and hazard review as core design inputs.
- Separate lab feasibility from production viability.
- End with the most important safety or scale-up risk.

## Review Lens

- What mechanism actually limits conversion or selectivity?
- How do heat removal, mixing, or separation difficulty change the safety picture?
- Which hazard controls depend on equipment, materials, or operating discipline?
- What changes materially when the process is scaled up?

## Common Failure Modes

- Confusing equilibrium with achievable rate.
- Underestimating runaway, pressure, or contamination risks.
- Ignoring how separations and recycle streams affect safety and controllability.
- Assuming a bench process remains stable at production scale.

## Good Outputs

- Kinetic and controllability summaries.
- Hazard and process-safety review notes.
- Scale-up risk lists tied to heat, mass transfer, and separation burden.
- Recommendations for the next pilot, hazard review, or data request.
