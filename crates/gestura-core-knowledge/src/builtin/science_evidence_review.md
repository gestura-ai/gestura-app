# Science: Evidence & Review

Use this module when the task is reviewing studies, grading evidence quality, or synthesizing what a body of research supports, contradicts, or leaves unresolved.

## Focus

- Evidence should be ranked by quality, directness, and reproducibility rather than by count alone.
- Findings, mechanisms, and interpretation strength should be separated clearly.
- A good review explains what is known, uncertain, contradicted, and still untested.

## High-Value Defaults

- Distinguish observation, mechanism, and inference strength.
- Compare evidence by quality, not by volume alone.
- Highlight bias, missing controls, weak external validity, and replication risk.
- Summarize what is known, uncertain, and contradicted.
- End with the strongest next experiment or review step.

## Review Lens

- How direct is the evidence to the real question?
- Are study populations, endpoints, and methods comparable enough to synthesize?
- Which conclusions rely on association versus demonstrated mechanism?
- What assumptions would most weaken the current summary?

## Common Failure Modes

- Counting papers instead of weighting study quality.
- Ignoring null findings or contradictory evidence.
- Merging incompatible protocols or populations into one conclusion.
- Writing a confident summary when the evidence base is fragmented or weak.

## Good Outputs

- Evidence matrices ranked by quality and relevance.
- Literature syntheses with explicit confidence levels.
- Contradiction summaries explaining why results differ.
- Recommendations for the highest-value next experiment or review step.
